Affiliate marketing is one of the most effective channels for growing on a limited budget. The principle is simple: partners recommend your offer through a personal link or code. If that leads to a purchase or booking, they earn a commission. You only pay for results.

This article is about getting started in practice: how to launch your first affiliate program in five steps, and what to watch out for legally in Austria and Germany. Why partner programs are such a strong growth lever overall is covered in our foundational article on partner programs.

Why Affiliate Marketing?

Performance-based: You only pay once a sale or an inquiry happens.

Scalable: Every new partner extends your reach without your workload growing at the same rate.

Measurable: Every click and every sale is attributed to a partner.

In your control: You decide who becomes a partner, what commission applies and how people talk about you.

Big brands are leaning in too. According to impact.com's report “The Global State of Affiliate Marketing 2025”, 74% of the brands surveyed increased their investment in affiliate and partnership marketing in 2024. What's behind this shift is covered in our article on partnership marketing.

5 Steps to Your First Program

1. Define your offer

What should your partners promote? A clear offer with a fixed price that can be explained in one sentence works best: an online course, a package, a first appointment, a product. Complex custom offers are harder to recommend and harder to track.

Check your margin too. The commission has to be attractive for partners and still profitable for you. Don't just calculate with the first purchase, but with what a client spends with you on average over time.

2. Choose a commission model

The most common models:

  • Percentage commission on revenue, typical for digital products and courses
  • Fixed amount per sale, easy to plan for services with fixed prices
  • Commission per lead, for example for a booked discovery call
  • Recurring commission for subscriptions and memberships, for as long as the client stays

Also decide how long a click stays attributed to a partner, what happens with cancellations and when payouts are made. A clear written model prevents disputes later and motivates more than a high but vague commission.

3. Choose a platform

A professional platform handles tracking, reporting and payouts. There are three ways to start:

  • Built-in features of your shop or course platform, which often include a simple affiliate program
  • Standalone affiliate software that you connect to your website and manage yourself
  • Partner platforms and networks that also give you access to existing publishers and creators

For your first ten partners, the simplest option is often enough. What matters is that every partner gets her own link or code so you can attribute results clearly. Discount codes have one advantage: they still work when visitors decline cookies.

4. Recruit partners

Start with your network: happy clients, peers with complementary offers, bloggers and creators who already reach your audience. Five to ten partners who truly believe in your offer bring more than a hundred who post your link once and forget about it.

Referrals work because they transfer trust. A long-term study of almost 10,000 new customers at a German bank found that customers acquired through a referral program were worth 16% more on average over the entire customer relationship than comparable non-referred customers.

5. Support and optimize

A partner program doesn't run itself. Give your partners everything they need: a short description of your offer, images, suggested copy and answers to common questions. Check in regularly, share results and say thank you. Your best partners deserve special attention, for example a higher commission tier or an exclusive offer for their community.

Legal Basics in the DACH Region: Disclosure and Privacy

Affiliate links are advertising, and advertising has to be recognizable as such. This mainly affects your partners, but it also affects you as the provider, because you should set out in the partner agreement how disclosure works.

  • Austria: Under Section 26 of the Media Act (Mediengesetz), paid content in periodical media, which includes websites, must be labeled “Anzeige”, “entgeltliche Einschaltung” or “Werbung”. Violations can be fined up to €20,000.
  • Germany: Under Section 5a(4) of the Unfair Competition Act (UWG), the commercial purpose must be recognizable. In October 2025, the Higher Regional Court of Munich ruled that affiliate links must already be labeled in an article preview.
  • Privacy: If your program uses tracking cookies, you need visitors' consent on your website. Also mention the partner program in your privacy policy.

Clear labels such as “Werbung” or “Anzeige” right next to the link or at the start of the post are on the safe side. Symbols or hidden asterisks generally aren't enough. This overview is not legal advice. If in doubt, have your partner agreement reviewed by a lawyer and clarify the tax treatment of commissions with your tax advisor.

The Most Common Mistakes When Starting

  1. Too many partners at once. Quality beats quantity. Better a few who really recommend you.
  2. Commission too low. If it isn't worth the effort for partners, your link goes unused.
  3. No materials. Partners who don't know how to talk about you won't talk about you at all.
  4. Not looking at the numbers. Without regular reporting you won't know which partners actually deliver. Which metrics matter is explained in our article on performance marketing.
